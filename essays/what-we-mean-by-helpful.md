A camera that "helps" by smoothing your face is less helpful than a camera that shows you your face. A map that "helps" by hiding streets you don't need is less helpful than a map that shows you the streets.

The word *helpful* gets used as a synonym for *opinionated*. They are not the same thing.

## The shape of over-helpful

Over-helpful tools have a tell: they add steps you didn't ask for.

- "Would you like me to also..."
- "I noticed you might want to..."
- "Here's a summary of what we discussed..."

Each of these is a small interruption. Each one is a guess the tool made about what you wanted. Sometimes the guess is right. Most of the time, you have to read past the guess to get back to what you were doing. The tool has inserted itself between you and your work.

## What restraint looks like

A helpful tool is one that does the thing you asked, then stops. If you ask for a sentence, it gives you a sentence. If you ask for an essay, it gives you an essay. The tool does not decide for you whether your sentence is too short, or whether your essay is too long.

Restraint is not absence. A good tool has strong defaults. A good tool makes the obvious thing easy. But a good tool does not *guess* at the non-obvious things and then act on those guesses as if they were instructions.

## A simple test

When you finish using a tool, do you feel you did the work, or do you feel the tool did it?

That's the test. Not "did the tool give me a good answer?" — that's a different question, and a smaller one. The question is about ownership. Did you walk away with something you made, or with something that was made for you?

The tools I trust most are the ones that leave me with my hands still on the wheel.
